Designed around a simple idea

The assistant should live on your machine.

01

Speak naturally

Voice is the primary interface. Say “Voxa”, ask, and it starts answering while it is still thinking—through one of 22 photoreal characters whose lips follow the words.

02

Run local models

Choose local AI through Ollama, llama.cpp or Strata. Keep everyday assistant work close to the machine and under your control.

03

See what it's doing

The screen always tells the truth: Ready, Listening, Thinking, Speaking or Paused, with explicit ACTIVE / PAUSE / OFFLINE control.

04

Get things done

Open and close apps, play music and video, browse, set reminders, get directions, or say “get me the cheapest ticket to Hawaii” and see the real search, cheapest first, from where you are.

WHAT YOU CAN SAY

Ask for it the way you would ask a person.

Apps and windows

  • “Open LibreOffice”
  • “Close Brutal Chess”
  • “Minimize all apps”
  • “Maximize yourself”

Names are matched by sound, and closing a browser by name closes only the one Voxa opened.

Music, radio and video

  • “Play Dirty Old Town by the Pogues”
  • “Play Bob’s Ska Radio”
  • “Pause the music”
  • “Stop music”

Plays in VLC with no ads; internet radio stations by name or genre.

Dictation and documents

  • “Start dictation”
  • “Clean up the text”
  • “Save file” — “What should I save the file as?”
  • “New document”

Types into the window in front, tidies what you wrote, and names and saves the file by voice.

Answers from the web

  • “What is the price of Bitcoin?”
  • “Who won the game today?”
  • “Tell me about Spaced Linux”
  • “Open that website”

Anything current is looked up first and answered from the results, with the source shown.

Places and travel

  • “Walking directions to the library”
  • “Nearest pharmacy”
  • “Cheapest ticket to Hawaii”
  • “Cheap hotel in Tokyo for four nights”

Opens maps and real searches, cheapest first, from where you are.

Your computer

  • “Update Spaced Linux”
  • “Install GIMP”
  • “How much disk space do I have?”
  • “What time is it?”

System questions are answered instantly from the machine, not guessed by a model.

Time and notes

  • “Set a timer for five minutes”
  • “Remind me at three to call Bob”
  • “Take a note: call the vet Friday”
  • “Give me my briefing”

Reminders are spoken when they fire; notes go to a plain text file in Documents.

Any program’s buttons

  • “Press Install in Spaced Bazaar”
  • “What buttons are there?”
  • “Read this window”
  • “Click Save”

Voxa presses real buttons in other programs, and asks you to type passwords yourself.

The full, living list — with a phrase to try for every feature and its current status — is in What Voxa can do.

SHE HEARS YOU, NOT HERSELF

Talk over the music. Interrupt her mid-sentence.

Voxa removes what your own speakers are playing before she listens, so a command still gets through with music or a video on. Say “Voxa, stop” while she is talking and she stops; her own voice never sets her off.

Choose your own wake word, pick from 22 characters, and let Voxa choose a speech model that fits your hardware on first run.

BUILT FOR THE DESKTOP

Not another browser tab.

Voxa is a native Linux application with a dedicated assistant shell, task state, model controls, voice input, and a renderer-independent avatar system.

One window, one face, three clear controls: ACTIVE, PAUSE and OFFLINE. What she is doing is always written on the screen.

LOCAL AI

Your hardware is part of the product.

Use the models that make sense for your machine. Voxa supports local backends today and is being structured so backend choice stays separate from the assistant experience.
