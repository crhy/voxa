Honeybees are small, busy insects that live in large colonies. A hive can hold tens of thousands of bees, each with a job. The queen bee lays the eggs that grow into new workers. Worker bees gather nectar and pollen from flowers and turn the nectar into honey. Bees communicate the location of good flowers by dancing in a figure called the waggle dance. Honeybees help plants reproduce by moving pollen from flower to flower. Many farmers rely on bees to pollinate their crops. Bee populations have been declining in recent years because of pesticides, disease, and loss of habitat. Protecting bees matters because they support both wild plants and the food people grow.
